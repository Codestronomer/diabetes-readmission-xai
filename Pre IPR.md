**Pre\_IPR**

**Section 1: Background Research and Literature Research (max 10 marks)**  
This section should be 2-3 pages long, with sub-headings such as Goals, Objectives, Background Research, Start by introducing your project, explaining your goals, objectives, and main research question (and any sub-questions). Ensure these questions align with your project’s goals and address the theoretical problem or challenge you're tackling. Your objectives should be specific, measurable, and linked to the theoretical outcomes you aim to achieve.

Provide a concise review of the relevant research you've conducted, focusing on key theories and academic frameworks. Explain how this research connects to your project and shapes both your current and planned work. Rather than listing many sources, critically analyse a few important studies (e.g., three or four) and demonstrate how they guide your theoretical approach. Discuss contrasting viewpoints or theories, and explain how your project builds on or differs from these ideas.

In addition, include examples of practical research to show how technical issues were resolved in your project. For instance, discuss how you applied certain methodologies or frameworks from the literature to address specific technical challenges. Highlight how the combination of theoretical research and practical problem-solving has informed your approach, providing concrete examples of how scholarly ideas translate into practical solutions.

Use this section to establish a strong foundation for both the theoretical and practical aspects of your project. Demonstrate how your background research supports your investigation and problem-solving, ensuring that your project contributes to both academic knowledge and practical applications. This balance will show how your work builds on theory while also resolving real-world technical challenges.

**Section 2: Summary of Progress to Date (max 20 marks)**  
This section should be 2-3 pages long and clearly outline the progress you've made so far, directly linking it to your project’s goals. Highlight completed tasks, such as literature review, defining requirements, creating datasets, building prototypes, or initial testing, and explain how they align with your objectives. Detail practical work initiated, such as software development, dataset creation, or model building. Describe the tools and technologies you're using, like Python for data analysis or Jenkins for testing, and justify why these are suitable for your project.

List specific deliverables, such as design documents, codebases, software prototypes, or test plans, and provide evidence of your progress, like screenshots, code snippets, or test logs. Reference these in the appendices. If you've encountered challenges, such as technical issues or unclear requirements, explain how you've handled them. Outline your project management approach, perhaps using a structured timeline for key milestones while maintaining flexibility for iterative development and testing. This section should clearly show measurable progress and your strategy for project completion.

**Section 3: Consideration of Ethical, Legal, Professional, and Social Issues (max 20 marks)**  
This section should be 1-2 pages long, reviewing the ethical, legal, professional, and social issues related to your project. Start by stating whether ethics approval is required, and provide a clear reason for your decision. Reflect on how this affects your research process.

Consider the current scope of your project while also thinking about how these issues could evolve if the project is further developed. Ethically, ensure your project respects privacy, avoids harm, and promotes fairness. Think about whether your methods might violate privacy standards and how you’ll manage these risks. Consider how these concerns might change if your project grows. Legally, make sure you comply with data protection laws like GDPR and respect intellectual property rights. Reflect on how handling sensitive data could become more complex if the project expands. Professionally, follow industry standards to ensure high-quality work and accountability. Think about how these practices support your project now and in future development. Socially, consider your project's impact on privacy, equality, or accessibility. For example, could your project create bias or promote inclusivity? Reflect on how these effects might grow with further development.

By discussing and reflecting on these issues now and in a possible future, you show awareness of your project's broader impact and will help add depth and academic value to your work.

**Section 4: Project Plan (max 15 marks)**  
This section should be 1-2 pages long. Explain your project management approach and discuss how (e.g., if you've adopted a hybrid method) aspects such as managing scope, time, resources, challenges, risk, quality, adjusting priorities, meetings, feedback, and report writing stays focused, on schedule, and meet the expected academic and professional standards.

List the key tasks that remain, including those you’ve already completed supported with planned target dates for each. Explain in summary what each task involves and the deliverables it will produce. Include your strategy for evaluating the quality of your work and how you will assess the overall project process and value of your findings. Remember to schedule time for writing the final report and preparing for any presentations or demonstrations. Regardless of your management approach, a Gantt chart\* is expected.

\*A Gantt chart visually represents project tasks along a timeline, showing when each task starts, its duration, and what task depends on another. It helps track progress and includes key milestones, highlighting the tasks that are critical to the project's completion. These charts make it easier visualise time and monitor progress \- however, they can become complicated for projects with many tasks and dependencies,  they may oversimplify relationships between tasks and make handling unexpected changes more difficult (requiring the need for regular updates to stay accurate). For the purpose of this report, your Gantt chart should accurately reflect your project at the time of submission.

**Section 5: Level of the Project (max 15 marks)**  
In this section, assess the level of your project by focusing on the problem, research, investigation, methods, and artefact. While some aspects may have already been covered in other sections, this section should serve as a critical reflection on why and how your project qualifies as a valid MSc level undertaking. This includes demonstrating the depth, complexity, and rigour expected, with a strong focus on testing, validation, and experimentation.

Begin by describing the challenge and why it interests you, linking it to your future career aspirations. Explain the artefact you’re developing (whether software, a model, or a system) and how it addresses the problem. Reflect critically on how the work demonstrates advanced skills and/or innovation, improves or diverges from existing technologies or approaches. Next, explore the depth of your investigation. Reflect on how you have or will comprehensively explore the research problem, combining both theory and practical application. Include plans for testing and validation, and critically assess the experiments you’ve conducted (or plan to conduct). Consider edge cases, feasibility, and performance under different conditions to demonstrate how your project will deliver meaningful, reliable results. Finally, justify the methods and tools you are using. Critically or comparatively evaluate why they are the best fit for your project and how they enable comprehensive testing and experimentation; show how these choices contribute to the reliability and effectiveness of your overall project.

By using this section to critically reflect on the project's depth, complexity, and rigour, you will effectively demonstrate that your project meets the expected academic and technical standards of an MSc.

**Section 6: Referencing and In-Text Citation (max 10 marks)**  
Use the Harvard referencing system consistently throughout your report, including all sources, even if they aren’t directly cited. List references alphabetically by the author’s surname. Ensure you use technical terms correctly and keep references relevant to your project. For in-text citations, follow Harvard guidelines by including the author’s surname and publication year. Ensure all citations support your project’s goals and demonstrate academic rigour. The references should reflect key studies, technical resources, and industry standards that informed your research and development. By using proper referencing and citations, you’ll enhance the credibility of your work and meet the academic standards expected for an MSc level project.

**Report Presentation  (max 10 marks)**  
Ensure your report is well-organised and properly named, with each section flowing logically into the next. Write clearly and concisely, allowing each section to build naturally on the previous. Check for spelling and grammar errors to maintain professionalism and readability. Follow good academic and professional standards in structure, citations, diagrams, illustrations, and examples. Support your arguments with well-justified reasoning and rationales.

A clear, well-presented report will help demonstrate your attention to detail and professionalism and make your ideas easier to understand and thereby potentially increase your academic mark.
